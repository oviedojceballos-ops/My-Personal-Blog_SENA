🌟 Introduction

Learning English has been one of the most important challenges and achievements in my life. As a software development trainee at SENA, I understand that English is not only a school subject; it is a key tool for my professional future. In this post, I want to share my progress, the difficulties I have faced, especially with pronunciation, and the goals I have set for myself.

Learning English is a journey that requires patience and practice.

📈 My Progress

When I started learning English, I felt insecure. I was afraid of making mistakes and I did not know many words. Today, I can understand written texts, follow simple conversations, and express my ideas with more confidence. I have improved my vocabulary, my grammar, and my ability to organize paragraphs.

One of my biggest achievements has been creating this blog in English. Writing about my life, my work, and my interests has helped me practice the language in a real and meaningful way. I also use Duolingo every day to review vocabulary and keep my learning habit active.

🏫 My Experience in SENA Modules 1, 2, and 3

The English modules at SENA have been very useful for my learning process. Each module focused on different skills and gave me tools to communicate better.

  • Module 1: I learned basic grammar, greetings, personal information, and how to introduce myself. This module helped me build a strong foundation.
  • Module 2: I practiced descriptions, routines, and simple conversations. I also learned how to talk about my family, my work, and my daily life.
  • Module 3: I worked on more complex topics such as opinions, experiences, and future plans. This module helped me connect ideas and write longer texts.

In each module, I completed activities such as written exercises, audio recordings, short presentations, and blog entries. These activities helped me practice reading, writing, listening, and speaking in an integrated way.

🗣️ Pronunciation Challenges

Pronunciation requires constant listening and repetition.

Pronunciation has been one of my biggest difficulties. Some English sounds do not exist in Spanish, and this makes them hard to reproduce. These are some of the specific challenges I have faced:

  • The "th" sound: Words like think, this, and three are difficult because the tongue must be placed between the teeth.
  • The "sh" and "ch" sounds: Words like ship and cheap require a distinction that does not exist in Spanish.
  • The "r" sound: The English "r" is softer and different from the Spanish "r", especially in words like right or really.
  • Word stress: Sometimes I place the stress on the wrong syllable, and the word sounds different.
  • Silent letters: Words like knife, hour, and island are confusing because some letters are not pronounced.

To improve, I listen to audios, repeat sentences aloud, and record my voice. This blog also includes audio recordings, which help me compare my pronunciation with the correct one.

🛠️ Strategies That Help Me

During this process, I have discovered some strategies that work well for me:

  • Practice every day: Even 10 minutes a day makes a difference.
  • Listen and repeat: I listen to short audios and repeat them to improve rhythm and intonation.
  • Use AI as support: I ask AI to explain grammar rules, suggest vocabulary, and correct my writing. But I always rewrite the suggestions in my own words.
  • Write real content: Creating blog entries about my life and interests helps me use the language meaningfully.
  • Ask for feedback: My teacher's corrections help me identify my mistakes and improve.

🎯 My Learning Goals

I have set clear goals for the future:

  1. Improve my pronunciation, especially the "th", "sh", and "r" sounds.
  2. Expand my technical vocabulary related to software development and IT.
  3. Speak with more fluency and confidence in conversations.
  4. Understand technical documentation and tutorials in English.
  5. Prepare for international certifications and future job opportunities.
  6. Continue writing in English to express my ideas clearly and creatively.

💙 My Conclusion

Learning English is not easy, but it is possible when we practice with discipline and motivation. The modules at SENA, my daily practice on Duolingo, and my blog have helped me make real progress. I know I still have a lot to learn, especially in pronunciation, but I am committed to improving every day.

For me, English is not just a subject; it is a bridge to new opportunities. I will continue studying, making mistakes, correcting them, and moving forward. Every step I take brings me closer to my goals.
